AI_ACT_READINESS_MAP

2026-03-28 5 MIN READ AI POLICY

The AI Act now has a clock teams can plan against. Some obligations have already applied since 2 February 2025, more took effect on 2 August 2025, and the wider framework applies from 2 August 2026.

WHAT_IS_ALREADY_LIVE

The prohibitions, key definitions, and AI literacy obligations have already applied since 2 February 2025. Governance structures and general-purpose AI model obligations followed on 2 August 2025.

WHY_TEAMS_NEED_AN_INVENTORY_NOW

The organizations most at risk are the ones that cannot say where AI appears in the product, what role they play in the chain, what models they depend on, and which use cases could qualify as high risk. That is not a legal memo problem. It is a product inventory problem.

Once the inventory exists, the rest of the program becomes more concrete: contract review, documentation, human oversight, testing, logging, and whether certain use cases belong in the product at all.

"You cannot prepare for the AI Act if the organization cannot first find its own AI."

THE_WORK_BEFORE_2_AUGUST_2026

The 2026 milestone is where many general obligations bite hardest for deployers and providers. Teams should use the remaining window to clarify scope, ownership, procurement evidence, and what internal controls already exist versus what still lives as tribal knowledge.

  • List every AI-enabled feature, internal tool, and third-party dependency used in production workflows.
  • Record whether the organization acts as provider, deployer, importer, distributor, or some combination.
  • Identify use cases that touch employment, education, public services, biometric processing, or other higher-risk categories.

For many teams, the practical question is not whether the Act exists. It is whether their AI features are governed with enough discipline to survive customer due diligence and internal review.

WHAT_TO_DO_NEXT

Start with a narrow, provable map rather than a huge speculative program. The fastest progress usually comes from choosing the top five AI-relevant flows, assigning owners, and documenting how the feature works today.

  • Create a product inventory with model sources, intended use, user groups, and failure impact.
  • Review contracts and procurement questionnaires for model or platform suppliers now, not during the next renewal panic.
  • Document oversight, escalation, and rollback paths before the product team needs them in anger.
