ACCESSIBILITY_AFTER_JUNE_2025

2026-03-28 7 MIN READ ACCESSIBILITY

The European Accessibility Act began applying on 28 June 2025. For frontend teams, that means accessibility is no longer a promised clean-up phase. It is a release condition.

CURRENT_BASELINE

The Act applies to covered products and services including e-commerce and a range of consumer digital services. For many teams, the visible impact lands in websites, apps, account journeys, and support-heavy forms.

WHAT_CHANGED_FOR_FRONTEND_WORK

The change is bigger than semantic markup or a one-time audit. Accessibility now needs to live inside the delivery system: component rules, content standards, QA scripts, sign-off checklists, and evidence collected before release.

Teams that still treat accessibility as a final specialist pass usually find the same problem: the defects were introduced much earlier by shared patterns, unclear ownership, and missing release gates.

"Accessibility failures are usually process failures before they become code failures."

WHERE_THE_WORK_SHOWS_UP

The most durable response is to treat accessibility as shared infrastructure. That means defining acceptable component behavior, testing it in the same places you test regressions, and giving product teams a repeatable way to prove the result.

  • Define accessible states for forms, dialogs, menus, notifications, and checkout steps in the design system itself.
  • Test keyboard flow, visible focus, zoom behavior, and error recovery in the real customer journeys.
  • Attach accessibility evidence to release signoff instead of treating it as a separate exercise after approval.

The teams improving fastest are not the ones with the prettiest audit deck. They are the ones whose shared components stop reintroducing the same problem under a new page name.

WHAT_TO_REVIEW_NEXT

If your service touches EU consumers, start with the flows that generate revenue, support tickets, or compliance anxiety. Those are usually the same places where inaccessible controls, weak focus handling, and unclear validation messages hurt people first.

  • Checkout, onboarding, login, password reset, account settings, and payments.
  • Reusable form controls, modal patterns, and any component that handles errors or time limits.
  • Mobile navigation, zoomed layouts, and content that relies on color or position alone.

PRIMARY_REFERENCES

  • Directive (EU) 2019/882, the European Accessibility Act
  • EUR-Lex summary of the European Accessibility Act
